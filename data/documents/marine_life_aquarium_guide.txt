ORBITAL AQUARIUM - MARINE LIFE FIELD GUIDE
Edition: 2026

1. CLOWNFISH
Clownfish are small reef fish commonly associated with sea anemones.
They can form a protective relationship with anemones, gaining shelter while helping remove food scraps and debris.
Clownfish can change sex during their lifetime. In a social group, the largest individual is typically female.
Their diet may include algae, small crustaceans, and food particles.

2. GIANT PACIFIC OCTOPUS
The giant Pacific octopus is one of the largest octopus species.
It has eight arms lined with suckers and can change color and skin texture for camouflage.
It is primarily active at night and feeds on crabs, shrimp, fish, and other marine animals.
It has a relatively short life span, commonly around three to five years.

3. SEA TURTLES
Sea turtles are marine reptiles that breathe air and must return to the surface to breathe.
Green sea turtles mainly eat seagrass and algae as adults.
Leatherback sea turtles have a diet strongly associated with jellyfish.
Female sea turtles return to suitable beaches to lay eggs.
Young hatchlings generally move toward the ocean after emerging from their nests.

4. MANTA RAYS
Manta rays are large filter-feeding rays.
They consume plankton and other very small organisms by filtering food from seawater.
Manta rays do not have stinging tails like stingrays.
They are often observed in warm tropical and subtropical waters.

5. CORAL REEFS
Coral reefs are ecosystems built largely by colonies of tiny animals called coral polyps.
Reef-building corals often live in partnership with microscopic algae that provide energy through photosynthesis.
Coral bleaching occurs when corals lose much of these symbiotic algae, often because of environmental stress.
Healthy coral reefs provide habitat for many marine species.

6. PENGUINS
Penguins are flightless birds adapted to marine environments.
They use their wings as flippers for swimming.
Emperor penguins are native to Antarctica and can endure extremely cold conditions.
Many penguin species feed on fish, krill, or squid.

7. MARINE CONSERVATION
The aquarium supports conservation projects focused on habitat protection, species monitoring, and public education.
Visitors can participate in the annual Ocean Cleanup Day held on the first Saturday of September.
The aquarium does not sell live marine animals to private collectors.
Questions about conservation programs should be directed to the Marine Science Department.
